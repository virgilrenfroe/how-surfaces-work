How Edges Turn to Mirrors

Look straight down into water and you see the depth. Look toward the horizon and the surface becomes a mirror. Metals tint that mirror; plastics keep it white.

F0 · Schlick

Specimen 03 — F0 slider

Head-on strength of the mirror

F0 is how much light reflects when you look straight at the surface.

Glass sits near 0.04. Metals sit much higher and often tinted. The curve below is Schlick’s simple Fresnel approximation.

After the lab

Where you see this

Fresnel and metal color are everyday tools for people who light water, paint, glass, and jewelry on screens and in stores.

  1. 01

    Wet streets and open water in film and games

    Look-development artist · lighting artist

    Look-development and lighting artists build water and wet roads so reflections grow toward grazing views. Without Fresnel, a lake looks painted on instead of like a surface you can skim with your eye.

  2. 02

    Edge highlights on car paint

    Automotive CGI artist

    Automotive CGI artists rely on Fresnel so clearcoat and body panels brighten at the silhouette. That edge glow is one reason a digital car still reads as lacquered metal under a moving light.

  3. 03

    Jewelry and copper cookware in online stores

    3D product visualization artist

    Product visualization artists give gold and copper a high, tinted F0 and almost no diffuse color. That is how rings and pans keep warm reflections instead of looking like yellow plastic.

  4. 04

    Glass façades that turn to mirrors

    Archviz artist

    Archviz artists set glass so towers look open when you face them and reflective when you see them at a shallow angle. Fresnel keeps those façades truthful in daylight renders for clients.

  5. 05

    Measuring how much light bounces

    Materials scientist · optical engineer

    Materials scientists and optical engineers measure reflectance across angles for coatings and glass. The rise toward grazing is the same Fresnel behavior students see in this lab.

For teachers

Short objectives, one classroom loop, and how Fresnel maps to studio and science practice.

Learning objectives

  • Predict that reflectance rises toward grazing angles.
  • State that everyday dielectrics sit near F0 ≈ 0.04.
  • Explain why metals tint reflections and lack diffuse color.
  • Describe how roughness softens the bright edge.

10-minute classroom activity

1) Look into a window from face-on, then from a side angle. 2) Note when the room or the outdoor scene dominates. 3) Open Specimen 01 and match the change. 4) Finish on Specimen 02: write one sentence on why gold reflections stay warm.

Art & design map

Wet-street lighting, car-paint edge glow, jewelry look-dev, glass façades in architecture images.

Materials science map

Fresnel equations, Schlick approximation, dielectric vs conductor F0, angle-dependent reflectance measurements.
