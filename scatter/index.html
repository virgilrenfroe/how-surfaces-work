How Soft Bodies Glow Inside

Some materials do not stop light at the surface. Light enters, scatters inside, and exits nearby. That soft glow is why wax and jade look alive under a lamp.

Time 20 min
You will learn Scatter · thickness · warm tint

Core lesson Light exits offset from where it entered

What you will see

ScatterHow far light travels inside before it fades. Longer paths make a softer glow.
Thin partsEars, leaves, and thin rims glow more than thick cores under the same backlight.
Warm tintLonger wavelengths often travel farther in tissue, so the exit light can look warmer.
OpaquePlastic of the same color stops light at the surface and stays dark from behind.

Thin · thick

Specimen 02 — Thin parts glow more

A thin rim lights up first

Under the same backlight, thin regions have a shorter path through the material, so more light exits.

That is why an ear rim or a leaf edge can glow while a thicker center stays darker. This is a classroom demo of scattering, not a medical claim.

Warm tint

Specimen 03 — Warm scatter tint

From white glow to warm exit light

In many tissues, longer wavelengths travel farther than shorter ones. The exit light can look warmer than the entry light.

Slide the tint toward warm red-orange to see that bias. Keep the note modest: it is a teaching picture of wavelength-dependent scatter, not a diagnosis.

After the lab

Where you see this

Subsurface scatter is everyday craft for people who light skin, candles, soap, and translucent plastics.

  1. 01

    Skin, ears, and noses in film and games

    Look-development artist · character artist

    Look-development and character artists dial scatter so ears and nostrils glow under backlight while foreheads stay softer. Without it, digital skin reads like painted rubber.

  2. 02

    Candles, soap, and milky plastics in product shots

    3D product visualization artist

    Product visualization artists use scatter so candles and soap bars glow from within. Clients compare those stills to real wax under a desk lamp before approving a mold.

  3. 03

    Tissue under light in teaching visuals

    Medical · scientific visualization artist

    Scientific visualization artists show how strong backlight can make thin tissue look warmer. They keep claims modest and stick to well-known classroom demos of scatter in soft media.

  4. 04

    Translucent plastics and soft-touch parts

    CMF designer · industrial designer

    CMF and industrial designers review milky plastics and soft-touch housings for how light passes through edges. Scatter distance changes whether a part feels cheap or premium under store lighting.

  5. 05

    Light transport in translucent media

    Materials scientist

    Materials scientists study how light scatters and attenuates inside wax, polymers, and tissue phantoms. Those measured transport lengths are the same idea students explore with the Scatter slider.

For teachers

Short objectives, one classroom loop, and how scatter maps to studio and science practice.

Learning objectives

  • Explain that scatter means light exits near, not only at, the entry point.
  • Predict that thin parts glow more than thick parts under backlight.
  • Describe a warm bias when longer wavelengths travel farther.
  • Contrast translucent wax with opaque plastic of the same color.

10-minute classroom activity

1) Hold a hand over a safe desk lamp and note which edges glow. 2) Open Specimen 02 and match thin vs thick. 3) On Specimen 01, write Scatter and compare wax to opaque. 4) Write one sentence on why the opaque block stays dark.
